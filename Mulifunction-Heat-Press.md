This multifunctional 5 in 1 heat press machine can transfer photos or text onto cotton, fibre, metal, ceramic, glass and other materials through thermal transfer.

[Main document about use - maintained on Google Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1fDQqWHGEJ8UmhO1nPdquTbKyhGeA_HGN/view?usp=sharing)

### Component Parts

- 12" x 15" heat platen for transferring onto T-shirts, Fabric etc
- Mug heating plate for transferring onto ceramic Mugs
- Cap heating plate for transferring onto Baseball type peaks
- Two plate heating plates for transferring onto ceramic plates
- Digital Dual display electronic control unit with LCD timer allows you presetting time and temperature control unit

### Operating

> **WARNING:** the various heating plates get very hot use extream care when operating and make sure the device is off before leaving.

The main heating plate and the mug plate have two cables which connect to the main control unit, the 4 pin cable supplies the heating element and controls the temperature, the two pin cable is for the microswitch, this is what activates the timer when the plate is compressed.

The Cap plate and the Plates plates do not have a microswitch so only have the 4 pin cable to connect.

Make sure the main control unit is switched off before connecting the cables, each cable plug has a different number of connectors and can only be connected to its corresponding socket.

The top display of the control unit displays the temperature of the plate being used and the bottom display show the time that has been set.

When you first switch the device on it will show the setting from the last time it was used.

### Set the controller as follows

Press the Set button - SP will appear in the top display, use the arrow buttons to increase and decrease the temperature seen in the bottom display to the required temp.

Press the set button - St will appear in the top display, use the arrow buttons to set the time required in seconds.

Press the set button - the top display will show C - F, use the arrows to select Celcius or Faranhight.

Press the set button - the displays now show the temperature at the top and time on the bottom you have set
(Note: if you have just switched on the machine then the plate will not have reached the required temperature and you will see the displayed temperature increase on the display until it reaches the set temperature.)

### Advance Settings

The advanced setting allows the plate to start at a pre-set temperature and then increase the temperature to a higher level when the plate is compressed when this higher level is reached the timer will start and after the pre-set time stop and sound the alarm.

This is required when printing onto mugs and plates as the cold mug or plate will initially reduce the temperature and needs time to reach the operating temperature.

## T-shirt Transfer

1. Create the artwork for transferring to the T-Shirt then print the image onto T-shirt Transfer paper as a mirror image
  
2. Set the controller to a temperature of 191 degrees C with a time of 18 secs
  
3. When the plate has reached the desired temperature pre-press the T-shirt for 3-4 secs to remove any creases
  
4. Place the transfer print image down on the T-shirt in the desired position.
  
5. Close the press, after the pre-set time the alarm will sound open the press and remove the T-shirt
  
6. Wait for about 30 seconds for the T-shirt to cool then peel off the transfer paper.
  

_The temperature and time may vary slighty depending on the make of Transfer paper, the above temperature and time was used with IBM T-shirt Transfer paper_


## Sublimation Coasters

| Setting | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Temperature | 190–210 °C |
| Time | 85 seconds – 2 minutes |

> **NOTE:** This guide is based on the MDF sublimation coasters from [Xpres](https://www.xpres.co.uk/unisub-coaster-pack-of-40) (SKU: XP4062). Other coasters or coaster materials may require different settings or timings.

To print sublimation coasters, you will be using the multi-function heat press (also used for T-shirts), as well as the mug sublimation paper and printer.

It is recommended that you print six coaster designs onto A4 at a time in order to save paper (2×3 grid).

> **NOTE:** Before beginning, it is recommended to use a piece of scrap A4 from the laser printer downstairs to test-print the artwork. This saves sublimation paper and can highlight any problems with the print or printer—such as the printhead needing cleaning after a period of inactivity. It is normal for colours to look odd when printed.

---

### Preparation

1. Create your artwork for transferring to the coasters, ideally using the 2×3 grid template.
2. Print this on the mug sublimation paper using the mug sublimation printer. The image should be reversed automatically.
3. At this point, it is strongly recommended that you cut the printed designs into three sets of two. It is easier to manage alignment and transport to the press, and if something goes wrong you’ve only ruined two coasters instead of six.
4. Place the printed paper design side facing up. Take a new sublimation coaster and place it face down onto the print. Do this for each coaster on the sheet.
5. Using heat-proof/Kapton tape (found on the shelf above the printer), carefully tape down the coasters to the paper. You don’t need much tape—just enough to hold each coaster in place and aligned. One or two strips across two coasters is usually enough.
6. Carefully transport your taped coasters to the heat press with the coasters facing upwards, so you are looking at the blank back of the sublimation paper. Swing the heat press top to the right to expose the lower platen. Do not try to slide the coasters in like a toaster.


### Printing

Turn on the heat press. It will take some time to warm up.

1. The temperature will initially stabilise at 170 °C. Press the **Set** button once. “SP” should appear. Press the up arrow until it is set to 190 °C.
2. Press the **Set** button twice more to exit the menu.
3. Wait for the temperature to stabilise at 190 °C.
4. Pull the clamp towards you and down firmly to engage the press. It will time for 60 seconds. Leave it closed for at least another 25 seconds after the buzzer sounds.
5. Release the clamp by pulling the lever up and backwards. Even if you have more coasters to press, it is advisable to turn off the press and carefully swing it to the right and out of the way (EVERYTHING is still very hot!).
6. Remove the paper-and-coaster “sandwich” from the press. Carefully peel the paper away from each coaster. This is easier if you use a metal ruler or the tip of scissors to lift a corner before pulling it with your fingers. **Be careful - the prints are still hot.**
7. Let the coasters cool without touching the printed surface. While the coasters remain hot, sublimation can continue, and touching them may affect the print.
8. Repeat this process for additional coasters.
9. Once you've finished printing, reset the temperature to 170 by pressing **Set** and using the arrow keys to reduce the temperature to 170C. Press **Set** two more times to exit the menu. You can now turn off the heat press.


## Mugs

1. Create the artwork for transferring to the Mug (approx. 92mm high x 230mm max width) then print onto Transfer paper as a mirror image (i.e. flipped horizontally) using the dye-sublimation printer (the Epson, on the shelf above the 3D printers)
  
2. Cut-out and tape the image around the mug using some pieces of Kapton tape (the transparent orange stuff) - make sure it is tight and level!
  
3. Hook up the cables for the mug-press. Set the controller to a temperature of 200 degrees C with a time of 300 secs (see instructions above)
  
4. When the press has reached the desired temperature, insert the mug and apply the clamp
  
5. Reset the controller to restart the count-down at 300sec
  
6. After the pre-set time the alarm will sound. Turn off the controller, open the press and remove the mug
  
7. Peel off the transfer paper (careful, it'll be HOT) and immediately slosh the mug in some cool water to cool/seal the surface
